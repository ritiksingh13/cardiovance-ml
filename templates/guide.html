Guide to the terms

Every question on the risk check explained in plain language: what it means, how it is measured, what the usual ranges are and what to enter. The ranges are general reference values, not a diagnosis.

Where to find your numbers

Most values come from one of four places. If you are missing a report, ask your doctor or a clinic to arrange the test.

ValueWhere it comes from
Blood pressureA blood pressure check at home, a pharmacy or a clinic
Cholesterol, fasting blood sugarA blood test report (lipid profile and fasting glucose)
Resting ECGAn ECG report, usually a printout with a short written summary
Heart rate, ST depression, ST slope, chest pain on exerciseA treadmill stress test report (also called TMT)

Blood pressure at rest

How hard blood pushes against your artery walls while you are relaxed. Unit: mm Hg (millimetres of mercury).

What the two numbers mean

A reading is written like 120/80. The top number (systolic) is the pressure when your heart squeezes. The bottom number (diastolic) is the pressure when your heart relaxes between beats. This tool needs only the top number.

How it is measured

  1. Avoid coffee, smoking and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand.
  2. Sit quietly for 5 minutes with your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
  3. Rest your arm on a table at heart level, with the cuff on your bare upper arm.
  4. Do not talk during the reading.
  5. Take two readings, one to two minutes apart, and use the average.

Usual ranges (top number)

CategorySystolic
NormalBelow 120
Elevated120 to 129
High, stage 1130 to 139
High, stage 2140 or more
Emergency rangeAbove 180. Seek medical help right away

Total cholesterol

A fatty substance in your blood. Too much can build up inside arteries and narrow them. Unit: mg/dL.

How it is measured

A blood test called a lipid profile. A nurse takes a small blood sample from your arm. You may be asked to fast for 9 to 12 hours first. The report lists several lines (LDL "bad", HDL "good", triglycerides). Enter the line called Total cholesterol.

If your report is in mmol/L, multiply by 38.67 to convert to mg/dL.

Usual ranges

CategoryTotal cholesterol
DesirableBelow 200 mg/dL
Borderline high200 to 239 mg/dL
High240 mg/dL or more

If you do not have this result

Tick "I don't have this result". The model then uses an average value, so the estimate will be less reliable.

Fasting blood sugar

The amount of sugar (glucose) in your blood after not eating. High levels can mean diabetes, which raises heart disease risk.

How it is measured

Do not eat or drink anything except water for at least 8 hours, usually overnight. Then a lab blood test, or a finger-prick glucose meter, measures your glucose. On a report it may appear as FBS or fasting plasma glucose.

Usual ranges

CategoryFasting glucose
NormalBelow 100 mg/dL
Prediabetes100 to 125 mg/dL
Diabetes126 mg/dL or more

What to select

The model asks one simple question: is your fasting value above 120 mg/dL? Choose Yes or No based on your report.

Chest pain type

Doctors group chest pain by how likely it is to come from reduced blood flow to the heart. That kind of pain is called angina.

Option on the formWhat it means
Pressure or tightness with effort, eased by rest (typical angina)A squeezing or heavy feeling in the chest that starts with physical effort or stress and eases within minutes of resting.
Discomfort that only partly fits that pattern (atypical angina)Feels similar, but does not match every part of the typical pattern.
Pain that is probably not from the heart (non-anginal pain)Often sharp, or linked to breathing, pressing on the chest or changing position.
I have no chest pain (asymptomatic)No chest pain at all.

How it is decided

There is no machine for this. A doctor decides by asking when the pain starts, how it feels, how long it lasts and what relieves it. If you are unsure, choose the closest description or ask your doctor.

Having no chest pain does not mean your heart is healthy. Heart disease can be silent.

Chest pain during exercise

Whether physical effort brings on chest pain, pressure or tightness.

Answer Yes if you get these feelings while walking fast, climbing stairs or during a treadmill test. Answer No if effort does not bring them on. A stress test report often lists this as "exercise induced angina" or "chest pain during test".

Resting ECG

An ECG (electrocardiogram) records the electrical signals of your heartbeat. "Resting" means it was taken while you lay still.

How it is measured

You lie down and a technician sticks small pads (electrodes) on your chest, arms and legs. The test takes a few minutes and is painless. The machine prints a wavy line, and the report usually includes a written result.

Options on the form

OptionWhat it means
NormalNo concerning pattern was seen.
Minor wave changes (ST-T abnormality)The ST segment or T wave of the heartbeat looks different from normal, for example inverted or shifted.
Thickened heart wall (LVH)Left ventricular hypertrophy: the main pumping chamber has thicker muscle, often after years of high blood pressure.

Highest heart rate reached

The fastest your heart beat during exercise. Unit: beats per minute (bpm). This is not your resting pulse.

How it is measured

During a treadmill stress test you walk while the speed and slope increase. A heart monitor records your pulse throughout. Look for "Max HR", "Peak HR" or "Maximum heart rate achieved" in the report.

A smartwatch reading is only a rough guide and is not the same as a supervised stress test.

ST depression

On an ECG, the "ST segment" is a flat stretch between two parts of each heartbeat. If your heart muscle is not getting enough blood during effort, this segment can dip below its normal level. Unit: millimetres (mm).

How it is measured

During the treadmill stress test, the ECG at peak exercise is compared with the resting baseline. The size of the dip is measured in mm. Look for "ST depression" or "ST change" on the report. A bigger number means a bigger dip. If the report shows no dip, enter 0.

ST slope

The direction the ST segment is heading at peak exercise.

OptionWhat it means
Rising (upsloping)The line tilts upward. This is usually a healthy response.
FlatThe line stays level. This can suggest reduced blood flow.
Falling (downsloping)The line tilts downward. This is the pattern doctors watch most closely.

You do not work this out yourself. It is written in the treadmill stress test report as "ST slope".

Glossary

Angina
Chest pain or pressure caused by the heart muscle not getting enough blood.
Artery
A blood vessel that carries blood away from the heart.
bpm
Beats per minute, the unit for heart rate.
ECG / EKG
Electrocardiogram, a recording of the heart's electrical activity.
Fasting
Not eating or drinking anything except water, usually for 8 hours or more.
LVH
Left ventricular hypertrophy, a thickened wall of the heart's main pumping chamber.
mg/dL
Milligrams per decilitre, the unit used for cholesterol and blood sugar.
mm Hg
Millimetres of mercury, the unit for blood pressure.
Stress test / TMT
A treadmill test that records your heart while you exercise.
Systolic / diastolic
The top and bottom numbers of a blood pressure reading.

This guide is for general understanding only. If you have chest pain, shortness of breath or dizziness, seek medical care right away.
